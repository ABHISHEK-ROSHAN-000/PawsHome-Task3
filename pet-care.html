Pet-care library

Prepare the home before the welcome.

General guidance for new adopters, grounded in recognised veterinary and safety sources. It does not replace an examination or advice from a qualified veterinarian.

New adopter

The first seven days

Keep the routine simple. Offer a quiet retreat, predictable food and toilet times, limited visitors, and gradual exploration. Avoid forcing affection or introductions.

General transition guidance; individual behaviour plans should be discussed with the rescue and a qualified professional.

Health

Vaccination is essential

WSAVA recommends core vaccination for all dogs and cats. Rabies vaccination is considered core where rabies is endemic, including India.

Source: WSAVA vaccination guidelines.

Health

Book an early veterinary review

Take available medical records and discuss vaccination, parasites, nutrition, sterilisation, identification, dental health, and any signs of illness.

A veterinarian should decide the schedule for the individual animal.

Safety

Children and pets need supervision

Teach children not to disturb eating, sleeping, hiding, or nursing animals. Avoid hugging, climbing, chasing, and face-to-face contact.

Source: AVMA dog-bite prevention guidance.

Daily care

Build a sustainable routine

Plan exercise, enrichment, toileting, grooming, food, rest, training, and companionship around age, species, health, and temperament.

There is no universal routine; adapt with professional guidance.

Public health

Respond correctly after a bite

Wash the wound immediately with soap and running water and seek medical care promptly. Rabies is preventable, but post-exposure treatment is time-sensitive.

Source: WHO India rabies information.

Home safety

Secure doors, windows, balconies, and leads

Newly adopted animals may bolt when frightened. Check barriers, identification, carriers, harness fit, and household escape routes before arrival.

Use equipment appropriate to the animal’s size and behaviour.

Nutrition

Change food gradually

Start with the current diet when possible, then change slowly under veterinary guidance. Sudden changes can cause digestive upset.

Special diets should be recommended by a veterinarian.

Behaviour

Use reward-based training

Reward desired behaviour, prevent rehearsals of unsafe behaviour, and seek qualified help for fear, aggression, separation distress, or resource guarding.

Avoid punishment-based methods that can increase fear or conflict.

This page provides general education only. Seek a veterinarian for health concerns and a qualified behaviour professional for serious behaviour issues.
